Short answer

The Teledyne 7500 and 7600 are multi-component infrared gas analyzers with a Modbus RTU port on RS-232 (RS-485 through an external converter). Each channel reports an integer concentration plus separate decimal-point and unit registers. This map covers all channel readings, range selection and the key/display command registers.

Integration notes: Teledyne Analytical Instruments 7500 / 7600 infrared gas analyzer (Modbus RTU)

Integration in brief

Read input registers 0-23 (7500) or 0-35 (7600) in one function-04 request; for each channel combine concentration, decimal position and unit.

Minimum registers to map

Addresses are 0-based wire addresses. Enough to monitor and run the unit; map more as the job needs.

Enable Modbus control & writes

Set the analyzer's station number in its communication settings menu; all other serial settings are fixed.

Byte order & addressing

  • Concentration needs the separate decimal register to scale.
  • Port is RS-232; use a converter for multi-drop RS-485.

Network setup

  • 9600 8N1 RTU; give each analyzer a unique station number.

Watch out for

  • Writing 64 (ZERO) or 128 (SPAN) to the keying register starts a calibration action.

Notes on this map

  • Concentrations are sent without a decimal point. Divide by 10^n where n is the channel's decimal-point register (0-3); the unit register gives vol%, ppm, mg/m3 or g/m3. Example in the manual: 1270 with decimal 2 and unit 0 = 12.70 vol%. Because the decimal place is per channel and range, the concentration registers are listed unscaled.
  • Relevant model per the address map: R = 7500, K = 7600. Channel registers CH1-CH8 are marked for both models; CH9-CH12 and the CH5 range-change setting are 7600 only.
  • Communication is fixed at 9600 bps, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit, RTU, over RS-232; up to 31 analyzers can share an RS-485 line through a converter.
  • Readings are transmitted even when the display shows over-range dashes.
  • Left out: the alarm/calibration status block from 30037 upward, whose rows are out of line in the source table, and the user-setting block 40001-40105 (calibration, alarm and timer settings).

Check it against the live device. A register map tells you what the controller can expose; the unit in front of you may differ by firmware or options. Read a few registers and compare them with the unit’s own display; for a 32-bit value the float and byte-order decoder shows all four word orders at once, so a wrong word order or scale is obvious. Questions about the Teledyne Analytical Instruments 7500 / 7600 infrared gas analyzer (Modbus RTU)

What Modbus registers does the Teledyne Analytical Instruments 7500 / 7600 infrared gas analyzer (Modbus RTU) have?

This map lists 43 registers: 36 input registers, 7 holding registers. 7 of them can be written. Registers vary with firmware, options and configuration, so confirm against the device.

Are the Teledyne Analytical Instruments 7500 / 7600 infrared gas analyzer (Modbus RTU) Modbus addresses 0-based or 1-based?

The manufacturer's document numbers registers Modicon 3xxxx/4xxxx (drop the prefix, subtract 1). Every address on this page is the 0-based wire address you put in the request; the documented number is shown under it where it differs. The manual prints a register number (30001, 40001, 42001) and a relative address (0000H, 07D0H) and says the relative address sent in the message is the lower four digits of the register number minus 1.

What are the minimum Modbus registers to integrate the Teledyne Analytical Instruments 7500 / 7600 infrared gas analyzer (Modbus RTU)?

Start with IR 0 CH1 concentration (CH1 concentration); IR 1 CH1 decimal point position (CH1 decimal position); IR 2 CH1 measurement unit (CH1 unit); IR 3 CH2 concentration (CH2 concentration); IR 4 CH2 decimal point position (CH2 decimal position).

How do I enable Modbus control of the Teledyne Analytical Instruments 7500 / 7600 infrared gas analyzer (Modbus RTU)?

Set the analyzer's station number in its communication settings menu; all other serial settings are fixed.
