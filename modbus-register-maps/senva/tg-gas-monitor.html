Short answer

The Senva TG is a wall-mount gas monitor with one or two gas sensors, a fan relay, an alarm relay and a buzzer, plus an RS-485 port that auto-detects BACnet MS/TP, Modbus RTU or Modbus ASCII. All data is in holding registers. This map covers communication setup, units, setpoints and hysteresis, sensor life and status, system state, relay and buzzer control, and the gas, temperature and supply-voltage readings with their statistics.

Integration notes: Senva TG series toxic/combustible gas monitor (RS-485 Modbus)

Integration in brief

Connect the TG to the RS-485 bus and poll; it learns the protocol, baud and parity from traffic. Its address is 100 + the last two serial digits. Read R320 (gas 1), R170 (state) and R210/R230 (relays) with FC 03; floats are high word first.

Minimum registers to map

Addresses are 0-based wire addresses. Enough to monitor and run the unit; map more as the job needs.

Enable Modbus control & writes

No setup needed: with Auto Protocol, Auto Baud and Auto Parity active (factory default) the TG adapts to the bus. Fix the settings via R122-R128 once the network is stable.

Byte order & addressing

  • Addresses are raw (R320 = address 320).
  • Floats and UINT32 are big-endian, high word first.
  • Writing R124 turns auto-baud off and cannot be undone remotely if the new rate is wrong.

Network setup

  • Default slave address = 100 + last two digits of the serial number.
  • Auto baud 9600-115200; parity even/odd/none detected automatically.

Watch out for

  • Writing 9699690 to R190 erases all configuration including the address.
  • With Auto Reset Statistics on, every read of a min/max/average resets it.

Notes on this map

  • Source host: the unaltered Senva guide hosted by a distributor; we did not find it on Senva's own site.
  • Word order: the guide says UINT32 and FLOAT use two registers in full network byte order, MSB first, and gives UINT32 byte examples, so high word first (ABCD).
  • BOOL registers are listed as UINT16 with 0/1 values. The default slave address is 100 plus the last two digits of the serial number; baud, parity and data bits are auto-detected by default.
  • Gas concentrations are FLOAT values; the guide gives setpoint defaults in ppm but does not print a unit on the concentration registers.
  • The source prints inconsistent value codes for R201 (4-8 for sensor 2 status) and R230 (4/5 for the alarm relay), so no value table is given for them.
  • Left out: User Data R294-301 (free storage) and Sensor calibration registers mentioned but not listed (R156, R166).

Check it against the live device. A register map tells you what the controller can expose; the unit in front of you may differ by firmware or options. Read a few registers and compare them with the unit’s own display; for a 32-bit value the float and byte-order decoder shows all four word orders at once, so a wrong word order or scale is obvious. Questions about the Senva TG series toxic/combustible gas monitor (RS-485 Modbus)

What Modbus registers does the Senva TG series toxic/combustible gas monitor (RS-485 Modbus) have?

This map lists 79 registers: 79 holding registers. 62 of them can be written. Registers vary with firmware, options and configuration, so confirm against the device.

Are the Senva TG series toxic/combustible gas monitor (RS-485 Modbus) Modbus addresses 0-based or 1-based?

The manufacturer's document numbers registers 0-based (the number printed is the address on the wire). Every address on this page is the 0-based wire address you put in the request; the documented number is shown under it where it differs. The guide prints raw protocol addresses prefixed with R (R320 = address 320); in Modicon notation that is 40321. 'R320/21' is a 2-register value starting at 320.

What byte order does the Senva TG series toxic/combustible gas monitor (RS-485 Modbus) use for 32-bit values?

Multi-register values in this map use ABCD (high word first (big-endian)). Set your client to this order for every 32/64-bit value in the table.

What are the minimum Modbus registers to integrate the Senva TG series toxic/combustible gas monitor (RS-485 Modbus)?

Start with HR 320 Sensor 1 gas concentration (Sensor 1 gas concentration); HR 330 Sensor 2 gas concentration (Sensor 2 gas concentration); HR 170 Current system state (System state); HR 210 Fan relay (Fan relay); HR 230 Alarm relay (Alarm relay); HR 158 Sensor 1 life (Sensor 1 remaining life).

How do I enable Modbus control of the Senva TG series toxic/combustible gas monitor (RS-485 Modbus)?

No setup needed: with Auto Protocol, Auto Baud and Auto Parity active (factory default) the TG adapts to the bus. Fix the settings via R122-R128 once the network is stable.
