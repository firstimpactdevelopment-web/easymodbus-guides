Acond Acond heat pumps (Modbus TCP interface v2.35) Modbus register map

Updated 19 November 2026

Short answer

Acond heat pumps act as a Modbus TCP slave on port 502 once a service technician has activated the function. Input registers report setpoints, sensor temperatures, a status bit field, the operating and regulation mode, fault codes and compressor speed or capacity. Holding registers let the master set room, hot water, pool and cooling targets, choose the mode, acknowledge faults and feed in its own room, outdoor, solar and pool temperatures.

Integration notes: Acond Acond heat pumps (Modbus TCP interface v2.35)

Integration in brief

Have Acond service enable ModbusTCP, tick ModbusTCP on the heat pump's web page, then poll input registers 30001-30024 with function 4 and write targets and TC_set at 40001-40014 regularly so the heat pump keeps using the master's values.

Minimum registers to map

Addresses are 0-based wire addresses. Enough to monitor and run the unit; map more as the job needs.

Enable Modbus control & writes

ModbusTCP is activated by an Acond service technician. Afterwards a ModbusTCP link appears on the heat pump's main web page; tick the ModbusTCP box in the dark blue bar to start communication.

Communication loss & watchdog

If no data is exchanged within MaxCommDataRefresh, the status shows 'ModbusTCP false' and values received from the master are replaced by the heat pump's own sensor readings until communication returns.

Byte order & addressing

  • Only one Modbus master may talk to the heat pump at a time.
  • Unit ID 2 is reserved for the heat pump cascade; the slave answers as ID 1.
  • A temperature written outside its range makes the heat pump use its own sensor value; an out-of-range setpoint is ignored.

Network setup

  • Modbus TCP on port 502; supported functions 3 and 4 for reading, 6 and 16 for writing.

Watch out for

  • The last communication error stays on the status page only until the next successful exchange.

Notes on this map

  • Acond's own site does not serve this description publicly (its older v2.34 link returns an HTML page); the link is an unaltered copy of Acond's PDF posted on the OpenEnergyMonitor forum.
  • Every value is a single 16-bit register: 'Int' rows are INT16 (several have negative minimums printed) and 'Word' rows are bit fields. x10 rows are tenths of a degree.
  • 30020, 30024 and 40014 carry compressor speed in rpm on most series but heat pump power in W on the PRO series; the units field is left blank for that reason.
  • Bit 5 of 30007 is printed with the same text as bit 4 (circuit 1 pump); it is reproduced as printed. Bits 11 and 13-15 are reserved.
  • The fault code books referenced by 30021-30023 are not part of this document.
  • 30019 is printed with a x10 modifier but a min/max of 10/25; the x10 scale is kept as printed.

Check it against the live device. A register map tells you what the controller can expose; the unit in front of you may differ by firmware or options. Read a few registers and compare them with the unit’s own display; for a 32-bit value the float and byte-order decoder shows all four word orders at once, so a wrong word order or scale is obvious. Questions about the Acond Acond heat pumps (Modbus TCP interface v2.35)

What Modbus registers does the Acond Acond heat pumps (Modbus TCP interface v2.35) have?

This map lists 38 registers: 24 input registers, 14 holding registers. 14 of them can be written. Registers vary with firmware, options and configuration, so confirm against the device.

Are the Acond Acond heat pumps (Modbus TCP interface v2.35) Modbus addresses 0-based or 1-based?

The manufacturer's document numbers registers Modicon 3xxxx/4xxxx (drop the prefix, subtract 1). Every address on this page is the 0-based wire address you put in the request; the documented number is shown under it where it differs. Registers are printed as 30001- (input registers, read with function 4) and 40001- (holding registers, function 3/6/16). Drop the leading 3 or 4 and subtract one: 30007 is input register 6, 40006 is holding register 5.

What are the minimum Modbus registers to integrate the Acond Acond heat pumps (Modbus TCP interface v2.35)?

Start with IR 6 TC_status (Status bits (on, running, alarm, defrost)); IR 13 rezim_pan (Operating mode); IR 9 T_act_air (Outdoor temperature); IR 8 T_act_water_back (Return water temperature); IR 20 err_number (Fault code); HR 0 T_set_indoor1 (Room setpoint circuit 1); HR 4 T_set_TUV (Hot water setpoint); HR 5 TC_set (Mode / fault reset bits).

How do I enable Modbus control of the Acond Acond heat pumps (Modbus TCP interface v2.35)?

ModbusTCP is activated by an Acond service technician. Afterwards a ModbusTCP link appears on the heat pump's main web page; tick the ModbusTCP box in the dark blue bar to start communication.

What happens when the Acond Acond heat pumps (Modbus TCP interface v2.35) loses Modbus communication?

If no data is exchanged within MaxCommDataRefresh, the status shows 'ModbusTCP false' and values received from the master are replaced by the heat pump's own sensor readings until communication returns.
